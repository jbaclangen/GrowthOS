## ROLE AND MISSION

Act as Braventa's founding brand strategist, product architect, SaaS product manager, UX strategist, design-system architect, marketing director, and technical documentation lead.

Your mission is to establish the complete brand identity, business definition, product specification, and persistent project knowledge system for Braventa, an AI-powered business growth platform that transforms digital marketing, CRM, content creation, website optimization, and business automation into connected, intelligent workflows.

This is not a request to create a logo alone. Build the foundational documentation and design system that will guide every future implementation of the Braventa application, marketing website, customer experience, documentation, community, emails, and marketing campaigns.

The resulting documentation must be authoritative, practical, implementation-ready, and reusable by future Claude Code sessions and other AI models.

## 1. BRAND FOUNDATION

### 1.1 Brand identity
- Brand name: Braventa
- Primary domain: https://braventa.io
- Business category: AI-powered business growth systems, marketing automation, and business operations intelligence.
- Business model: Subscription-based software combined with optional implementation, onboarding, and managed services.

Braventa is being developed from the GrowthOS business concept. GrowthOS describes the underlying methodology and vision; Braventa is the brand that will deliver that vision. Do not automatically use GrowthOS as the public-facing product name. Treat it as the original working concept unless the existing repository establishes a different approved product naming decision. Do not append "AI" to Braventa by default. The brand should be able to remain relevant as AI technology evolves.

### 1.2 Brand vision
Help businesses operate more intelligently by connecting their marketing, customer relationships, digital presence, data, and repetitive operational processes into one coherent system.

### 1.3 Brand mission
Make sophisticated marketing systems, CRM operations, business intelligence, and automation accessible to businesses that cannot afford large internal marketing, technology, and operations teams.

### 1.4 Core promise
Braventa helps businesses understand what needs to happen, turn that understanding into an actionable plan, execute repetitive work through AI-assisted workflows, and improve their systems using data and feedback.

### 1.5 The problem Braventa solves
Many small and growing businesses operate through disconnected tools, manual processes, inconsistent marketing, incomplete CRM data, and fragmented reporting. Their problems include:
- Marketing activities are disconnected from sales and revenue objectives.
- Business owners do not know which marketing activities deserve priority.
- Content production consumes significant time without a coherent strategy.
- Leads are lost because follow-up processes are inconsistent.
- CRMs become poorly maintained databases instead of operational tools.
- Websites attract visitors but fail to convert enough of them into leads or customers.
- Business information is scattered across websites, documents, spreadsheets, CRMs, and conversations.
- Owners pay different vendors for websites, content, SEO, advertising, automation, and CRM implementation, with limited coordination.
- Reporting explains what happened but does not clearly recommend what to do next.
- AI tools generate disconnected outputs but rarely execute an integrated business process.

Braventa must address these underlying operational problems rather than positioning itself as another generic AI writing application.

### 1.6 Target customers
Primary audience:
- Small and medium-sized businesses.
- Service-based businesses with repeatable lead-generation and follow-up processes.
- Owner-operated companies without large internal marketing or operations teams.
- Businesses already using websites, CRMs, email, social platforms, or other business software.
- Businesses that need better systems but cannot justify hiring a full team of specialists.

Potential early verticals: home and trade services; solar and electrical contractors; construction and related professional services; marketing-dependent local businesses; other service businesses with repeatable sales and customer acquisition workflows. These are potential segments to validate, not confirmed exclusive target markets. Do not restrict Braventa to one vertical without evidence or an explicit decision.

### 1.7 Brand positioning
Braventa is not merely: an AI content generator; a social media scheduling tool; a CRM replacement; a website builder; an automation agency; a chatbot with access to business information.

Braventa is an intelligent business growth system that connects these capabilities into coordinated workflows. The differentiation is the combination of business context, strategic recommendations, AI-assisted production, connected tools, workflow execution, measurement, and continuous improvement.

### 1.8 Brand personality
Intelligent, without being pretentious. Capable, without being intimidating. Modern, without following disposable AI trends. Premium, without being inaccessible. Strategic, without being unnecessarily complicated. Practical, without looking generic. Trustworthy, transparent, and business-oriented. Avoid exaggerated claims, empty AI jargon, aggressive hype, and unrealistic promises of effortless growth.

### 1.9 Brand messaging
Develop and document: one-sentence company description; short and long company descriptions; core value proposition; elevator pitch; homepage headline and supporting statement; product descriptions; audience-specific value propositions; key benefits and differentiators; objection-handling messages; calls to action; brand story; mission, vision, and values; short descriptions for social profiles, app listings, email signatures, and community pages.

Core positioning concept: *Turn disconnected business activities into one intelligent growth system.*
Potential messaging direction: *"Braventa connects your marketing, CRM, content, and automation into a smarter way to run and grow your business."*
Treat these as a strategic starting point; refine them into a coherent, distinctive messaging system. Do not promise guaranteed revenue, guaranteed rankings, guaranteed leads, or fully autonomous execution unless the product demonstrably supports those claims.

## 2. THE BRAVENTA BUSINESS OPERATING METHODOLOGY
Lifecycle: 1. Understand the business. 2. Identify opportunities and bottlenecks. 3. Establish priorities and objectives. 4. Build a strategic action plan. 5. Generate the required marketing and operational assets. 6. Execute tasks through connected systems and AI-assisted workflows. 7. Measure results. 8. Learn from outcomes. 9. Improve the strategy and workflows.

### 2.1 Business intelligence layer
Progressively understand a customer's business model and services; target customers and buying motivations; brand identity and tone of voice; products, offers, and differentiators; website and digital presence; customer acquisition channels; sales processes and CRM structure; existing marketing assets; connected applications and workflows; business goals, constraints, and priorities; available performance data. Use structured business profiles, approved source materials, and retrieval rather than repeatedly asking the AI to rediscover the business. Distinguish between verified information, customer-provided information, inferred insights, and AI-generated recommendations.

### 2.2 Intelligence-to-execution layer
Convert understanding into growth opportunities; website and conversion recommendations; SEO priorities; content strategies and calendars; campaign briefs and marketing assets; lead-management improvements; CRM workflow recommendations; automation opportunities; follow-up tasks; performance summaries and next actions. The platform should not stop at producing recommendations when an appropriate connected workflow can execute the next step. However, never claim that an integration, automated action, or publishing capability exists until it has actually been implemented and tested.

### 2.3 Continuous improvement layer
Eventually compare planned activities with actual results, identify underperforming workflows, recommend improvements, and help customers prioritize the next best action. Make the distinction between current MVP functionality and future capabilities explicit.

## 3. PRODUCT BLUEPRINT
- **A. Business Intelligence** — onboarding and profile; website and business information analysis; audience and offer understanding; brand voice and messaging profile; business knowledge base; strategic recommendations.
- **B. Marketing Strategy** — growth assessment; opportunity identification; campaign planning; objectives and priorities; content strategy; action plans and recommendations.
- **C. Content Studio** — calendars; social posts; blog/article drafts; email campaigns; video scripts and creative briefs; CTAs; repurposing; brand voice consistency; AI quality checks and approval workflows.
- **D. Website Intelligence** — website analysis; SEO recommendations; content quality assessment; CRO opportunities; UX observations; prioritized improvement plans.
- **E. CRM Intelligence** — CRM integration; lead and pipeline summaries; data quality observations; follow-up recommendations; workflow opportunities; reporting and insights. HubSpot is an initial priority (founder's existing experience). Do not assume Braventa replaces HubSpot; the direction is to make existing tools work together more intelligently.
- **F. Automation** — workflow templates; trigger-based processes; scheduled jobs; AI-assisted task execution; approval steps; execution logs; error handling and retries; notifications and follow-up tasks.
- **G. Analytics and Optimization** — marketing activity reporting; workflow performance; content production and approval status; CRM and lead insights; AI usage and costs; data-based recommendations.

Classify each capability as **MVP**, **Next phase**, or **Future vision**. Prioritize the smallest set of capabilities that can deliver measurable customer value.

## 4. PRODUCT AND BUSINESS MODEL
1. Customer discovers Braventa. 2. Signs up and creates a business profile. 3. Connects supported tools or supplies business information. 4. Braventa develops an initial business intelligence profile. 5. Customer receives actionable recommendations. 6. Uses available modules and AI-assisted workflows. 7. Reviews, approves, and executes work. 8. System records activity and available performance information. 9. Continues through a subscription, subject to plan limits. 10. Higher-value implementation or managed services can be purchased when needed.

Potential revenue streams: monthly/annual subscriptions; onboarding and implementation; CRM configuration and optimization; advanced automation packages; website and conversion optimization; managed marketing operations; premium integrations or usage allowances. Do not finalize pricing, entitlements, or service commitments without an explicit decision. Create a configurable pricing framework and label unvalidated assumptions.

## 5. BRAND IDENTITY AND VISUAL DESIGN SYSTEM
Modern, premium, intelligent, trustworthy B2B identity communicating clarity, connected systems, intelligent execution, momentum, control, practical outcomes. Avoid generic AI branding, excessive gradients, robot imagery, glowing brains, decorative dashboards, template SaaS visuals. Distinctive wordmark and symbol that work small, in nav bars, as app icon, in docs, in monochrome. Preserve specs in reusable files — a raster logo is never the only source of truth.

Required specs: primary/secondary logos; variations and clear space; incorrect usage; palette with HEX; light and dark themes; semantic colors; typography and type scale; spacing; layout and grid; radii, borders, elevation; iconography; illustration and photography; data visualization; button/card/form/navigation conventions; accessibility incl. contrast; responsive rules; motion. Implementable through reusable design tokens and components. Document rationale for colors, fonts, and visual details.

App UI principles: show what needs attention and what to do next; keep business context accessible; explain AI recommendations; distinguish drafts, approved content, running workflows, completed tasks; clear integration and execution status; transparent usage and subscription info; actionable errors; no vanity-metric dashboards; keyboard and accessible interactions; responsive desktop and mobile. Marketing site may be more expressive than the app; both must clearly belong to the same brand.

## 6. BRAND VOICE AND CONTENT RULES
Voice: a knowledgeable growth and systems consultant explaining complex technology in clear business language. Use specific benefits, concrete explanations, clear next steps, realistic claims, active language, helpful/confident/professional tone. Avoid buzzwords, "revolutionary AI" claims, unsupported guarantees, excessive emojis, artificial enthusiasm, unnecessary technical language, and presenting planned capabilities as live.

Document voice adjustments for business owners, marketing managers, sales/CRM teams, technical administrators, agency partners, developers, community members, existing customers.

Reusable copy templates (with `{{first_name}}`, `{{business_name}}`, `{{product_name}}`-style placeholders) for: website headlines and page descriptions; product/feature descriptions; signup and onboarding screens; empty states and errors; notifications; transactional emails; welcome emails; onboarding sequences; product updates; feature announcements; marketing campaigns; educational newsletters; customer success emails; support responses; community announcements; documentation introductions; social posts; sales presentations and proposals. Separate marketing from transactional/account emails. Account creation does not imply marketing consent.

## 7. TECHNICAL CONTEXT AND IMPLEMENTATION PRINCIPLES
Initial context (subject to repository inspection): marketing site hosting Hostinger; automation via self-hosted n8n on Oracle Cloud; Supabase for data/backend; OpenRouter for model routing with interchangeable providers; Claude Code and Claude Pro for development; HubSpot as first CRM; WordPress CMS experience; Canva for design.

Architectural separation: Frontend (app + dashboards); Supabase (data, auth, storage, permissions, business knowledge); n8n (workflow execution, scheduling, orchestration); AI routing layer (task-specific model selection, fallback, cost tracking); external integrations (CRM, analytics, content, communication, billing). Document discrepancies with the repository and recommend changes before disruptive decisions.

AI cost and quality: cheap models for extraction/classification/routine transforms; mid-tier for standard content and structured analysis; strong reasoning models for strategy, hard troubleshooting, critical reviews; deterministic code for calculations and rules; reuse business knowledge and cache; track model, tokens, latency, failures, cost; configurable fallbacks; evaluate quality before moving workloads to cheaper models; minimize customer data sent to models; never expose provider keys in browser code; keep model selection configurable.

Security and trust: tenant isolation; Supabase RLS; server-side secrets; protected connected-account credentials; logging of sensitive operations; approval for consequential actions unless explicitly configured; treat customer content and retrieved documents as untrusted input; respect privacy, email, and platform policies; document limitations, data handling, deletion.

## 8. CLAUDE CODE MEMORY AND PERSISTENT PROJECT KNOWLEDGE
Create a persistent documentation system (CLAUDE.md, README, docs/brand, docs/product, docs/architecture, docs/marketing, docs/decisions, design-system, public brand assets), adapted to the real project without duplicates.

CLAUDE.md must tell Claude Code to: (1) read relevant brand/product docs before user-facing work; (2) treat approved specs as source of truth; (3) keep naming, positioning, voice, tokens, UI conventions consistent; (4) never invent capabilities or present planned as implemented; (5) check MVP scope before adding features; (6) use reusable components and tokens; (7) follow the repo's architecture and conventions; (8) preserve tenant isolation, security, accessibility, privacy; (9) update docs when behavior/integrations/architecture/branding change; (10) record significant decisions in the decision log; (11) surface conflicts between code, requests, and docs; (12) not rewrite brand strategy or positioning without authorization; (13) prefer small, testable changes; (14) verify behavior before reporting completion. Point to detailed docs rather than duplicating them.

Source-of-truth hierarchy: 1. Explicit, recent founder decisions. 2. Approved product and brand specifications. 3. Approved architectural decisions. 4. Current implementation and verified system behavior. 5. Unapproved proposals and future roadmap ideas. Explain conflicts before rewriting foundational decisions. Never misrepresent the implementation because a spec says a feature should exist.

Documentation maintenance: update docs on material change; record decisions; update roadmap/MVP status; keep README accurate; add template examples; remove superseded guidance; avoid contradictory copies.

## 10. EXECUTION PROCESS
Inspect → establish brand and product definition → create persistent project knowledge → establish visual identity → create communication kit → apply and validate → report. Distinguish completed, partial, proposed, unresolved, and work requiring external credentials or approval. Never claim trademarks registered, legal availability verified, services deployed, accounts connected, or assets created that were not.

## 11. OPERATING RULES
Don't ask the founder to repeat information already available. Ask only when an unresolved decision materially affects brand, architecture, or product; otherwise make a labelled provisional recommendation. Don't over-polish minor branding before positioning is coherent. No expensive services without justification. Optimize for an MVP testable with real paying customers. Preserve flexibility without building every planned capability. Keep the brand consistent. Brand documentation is not legal clearance — never claim Braventa is trademark-cleared. Don't assume integrations exist. Treat specs as evolving version-controlled assets.

## FINAL ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
1. A new Claude Code session can read CLAUDE.md and quickly understand Braventa. 2. Docs explain what Braventa is, who it serves, which problems it solves, and how its methodology works. 3. MVP vs future vision is explicit. 4. Visual identity is documented in an implementable design system. 5. Marketing and product communication can be generated consistently from reusable guidelines. 6. The repository has one clear source of truth. 7. Decisions and assumptions are recorded. 8. Deliverables are verified and the handover report is accurate.
